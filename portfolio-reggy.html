Case 01 · Maritime IoT · Product Manager

From four inboxes to three products: the decision framework behind VMS, BuMS, and CTMS

Requests arrived from four directions at once — vessel monitoring, bunkering, cargo, and port surveillance inputs — plus market insight and an upcoming-project list. Meanwhile the grand plan was mapped but development kept getting interrupted by adhoc work. Nobody could answer which requests deserved a build, which deserved a buy, and which deserved a hold.

I put the whole triage on one Whimsical board: intake from all four sources plus market insight, a synthesis step owned by the product team, then a Product Decision Framework with two tracks. Core-based development asks build-from-scratch, modify-existing, integrate pre-made, hold, or scratch. Fast delivery asks vendoring-separately, use-released-feature, integrate pre-made, hold, or scratch. Both tracks converge on one gate — business and engineering needs — and exit as either Product Deliverables or Core Tech Ready to Implement. My own notes on that board say it plainly: find clarity on incoming projects, list what users actually need, and fix the adhoc jam.

That framework is what produced three products instead of one fuzzy platform. VMS v1.0 is fully Released. VMS v2.0 is Deployed on pilot vessels going through a three-month reliability trial. BuMS v1.0 is Deployed as a proof of concept on a pilot vessel, with automatic valve control still in Testing because there is no test rig for output control. CTMS v1.0 sits in Research, gateways and sensor integrations mapped but not started. Three P3 bets are Developing but honestly on hold: wind-data NMEA waits on a sensor that is not available, power monitoring waits on PCB budget, and the NMEA multiplexer waits on a purchase order that does not exist yet.

Case 02 · Maritime IoT · PM / Project Manager

Thirty initiatives, one lifecycle: how the PM column actually runs them

Fifteen items in the old model became thirty in the real log — MI-26-000 through MI-26-029, mixing RnD, product development, and project delivery. Every status meeting still asked the same three questions: how long, who is blocked, what ships first. The Whimsical board gave me the answer in one column: my Product Manager lane runs requirement into lifecycle management, then UAT external, POC, stakeholder mapping, timeline managing, and facilitation into engineer, product, and tribe — with a named document at every step.

Beside that lane sits the Product Owner rituals that keep delivery honest: backlog with acceptance criteria and user stories, sprint planning and review, internal UAT, product research and analysis. Upstream of both, the research chain feeds us: the operation manager drives data product and analytics into technical research, the analyst feeds technical research directly. Nothing enters the lifecycle without passing through that chain first.

The Initiative Log proves the lifecycle is loaded. Four deliveries are Done — Vanguard fuel monitoring (FAT done, installed early September), a second vessel delivery, a Megawati proof of concept (FAT done, install date pending), and the Ligo fuel-stick integration closed in Sprint 29. Five are In Progress — power monitoring with alerts, a Bumi Nusantara fuel install, the 4-20 mA receiver gateway, automatic valve control, and the AIO module evaluation. One flagship is New and fully gated: the Palu Sipat bunkering automation, milestones M0 through M7, October to mid-January. The rest sit in Hold or Initiating with the reason written down — no sensor in the office, no client need yet, use case still unclear. That honesty is the milestone system working.

Case 03 · Maritime IoT · Project Manager GATED — STARTS 26 OCT 2026

One bunkering automation, eight gates: the Palu Sipat M0–M7 run

The flagship delivery in the log is a bunkering automation for a Patra Niaga tanker — pressure and viscosity sensing, pressure-based valve loop control, 3-phase pump start/stop, then field testing and commissioning aboard. It sits at New / Ready for Review, which is exactly why it belongs in a portfolio: the plan is fully gated before a single wire is cut.

The Timeline tab runs it as eight gates from late October to mid-January. M0 evaluates and selects the 0.01 bar pressure/vacuum transmitter plus viscosity sensor in three days. M1 issues the sensor PO in a day. Then a three-week vendor lead-time gate the plan refuses to hide: firmware cannot start reading sensors until they arrive on 23 November. Panel design and fabrication (tasks T8–T11) run in parallel with the wait instead of after it. M3 implements firmware and software — sensor reading, calibration, valve loop, pump control, logging, data contract, HMI support (T12–T19, T33, T34). M4 bench-tests the pressure loop at the shop with fault injection. M5 installs panel, sensors, and wiring aboard over three days. M6 field-tests under real bunkering simulation and tunes the logic. M7 closes with customer UAT, as-built docs, and data-contract handover.

Around it run three parallel proof tracks on the same Timeline: the power-monitoring PoC, a CAN parser for marine panels, and an 8-channel 4-20 mA to Modbus gateway — each with its own bench test before anything goes near a vessel. The Master Backlog behind all of this is 193 rows by 21 columns, flowing through Sprint 28 to 33 tabs, every task carrying manhour, story point, role, module, acceptance criteria, definition of done, and sprint.

Case 04 · Agri IoT eFeeder · Researcher / BA

Satisfaction was high. Renewals were not. I found out why.

4,405 farmers used the feeder out of 375,384 registered — 1.17 percent. Of those, 1,922 churned. Trial signups looked great, then payment time came and people walked away. I designed the questionnaire, ran Q2 with 288 and Q3 with 334 farmers, and ran the regressions. Product satisfaction multiplied resubscribe odds by 1.8, price by 1.3, billing by 1.141. Service politeness did not move retention. The uncomfortable finding: 92 percent of churn came from free-promo orders.

Case 05 · Agri IoT eFeeder · Researcher / BA

Farmers kept rewiring the feeder. I measured the effort instead of blaming them.

Farmers kept modifying the unit, what diffusion research calls reinvention. I ran 58 exploratory questions plus 6 CES questions with 242 farmers. CES came out 20.7. Fourteen farmers did not understand basic Android use, about 40 percent of app operation was still done by field staff, and one pure product bug stood out: 35.9 percent of ponds were named with cobox initials, often by staff for convenience, so the system read two cobox IDs and timed out. The move-feeder feature had literally never been touched by a farmer.

CES = %EASY − %DIFFICULT = 20.7  ·  N=242

Case 06 · Agri IoT eFeeder · Researcher / BA

What a feeder for Rp50 ribu ponds actually looks like

The main feeder fit big ponds. The mass market lives below 150 square meters — median in my sample 5 ponds at 94 square meters. They tried it on promo, did the math, and quit. One catfish farmer told me near harvest he refills constantly while still paying pond hands, so he pays double. I interviewed 8 mass-market users and 11 farmers across four regions about competitors, then wrote three spec directions: cheaper smaller anti-clog unit, operability a low-tech user survives, and cultivation intelligence that earns the phone app.

Case 07 · eFishery R&D · Project Strategic Associate · Jul 2024 – Feb 2025

Research kept leaking. I gave it 11 stages and automated the nagging.

I joined Research and Engineering when every project lived in someone's head. Submission was a Slack message here, a Doc link there, a Jira card nobody updated. Projects got stuck in buffer planning with no manpower and went dormant. Head review had no reminder. My job was to make the whole thing visible and auditable without adding bureaucracy.

I mapped all 11 stages from submit to closed, tied each stage to ISO 9001 Plan-Do-Check-Act, then automated the nagging with Slack, Jira, and Apps Script — routine listings with days-on-card, auto-generate the eval doc and attach it to the card, auto-screen completeness, notify the head-review buffer.
